How to become a
worship leader

Great worship leaders are worshippers first and musicians second. They help a church meet with God, and that takes heart, skill and leadership growing together.

What a worship leader really does

On Sunday it looks like singing and playing. During the week it is planning sets, choosing songs that are true to Scripture, rehearsing a team, caring for musicians, working with production, and praying for the church.

Skills worth building

  • Musicianship: steady time, a solid vocal or instrument foundation, and the ability to lead a band.
  • Theology: knowing why we sing what we sing, and choosing songs that teach people well.
  • Leadership: communicating clearly, building a team, and handling hard conversations kindly.
  • Pastoral care: seeing the people on the team and in the room, not just the setlist.

How to get started

Join your church's worship team in whatever role is open, practice consistently, ask a worship pastor to mentor you, and study the Bible and theology alongside your music. Leadership grows by serving faithfully in small things first.

Common questions

Do I need to be an experienced musician?

No. Students come with different skill levels. A willingness to learn and serve matters most.

Is worship training only for future worship pastors?

No. Musicians, vocalists, production volunteers and anyone who wants to serve their church in worship can benefit.

Where is the program?

Dreamer's Bible College is in Austin, Texas, and connected to Dreamer's Church.
